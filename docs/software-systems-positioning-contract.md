# Software & Systems Engineering Positioning Contract

## Purpose

This contract repositions the portfolio from a primarily developer-forward presentation to a full-experience-forward presentation. Software and systems engineering become the organizing domain while factual career-title, education, skill, and evidence boundaries remain intact.

This document is the source of truth for Slices 17B through 17F.

## Approved Primary Identity

> Software & Systems Engineering

This is portfolio/domain positioning. It is not a claim that Hunter currently holds the formal employment title "Software Engineer" or "Systems Engineer". Verified historical employment titles remain governed by career-history evidence.

## Approved Supporting Position

> Computer Science student combining modern software development with more than two decades of technical experience across avionics, automated test systems, semiconductor equipment, industrial telemetry, controls, and systems troubleshooting.

"Computer Science student" must remain current until the degree is actually completed. The "more than two decades" claim applies to the broader technical career, not software engineering. Historical technical depth must not be relabeled as decades of software engineering.

## Narrative Model

Hunter's software work builds on, rather than replaces, a long technical career involving complex electronic, industrial, avionics, test, telemetry, control, and equipment systems.

The site should not frame the career as:

- technical work before software in a way that makes the prior experience seem irrelevant;
- a junior developer identity that ignores prior technical depth;
- a field-service or technician identity that subordinates current software capability.

The relationship should be cumulative:

technical systems experience -> systems/integration perspective -> computer science/software engineering capability.

Do not invent chronology, titles, dates, or outcomes.

## Three Evidence Pillars

### Software Engineering

Current evidence may include project-backed application development, backend systems, databases, authentication and authorization, testing, CI, deployment, monitoring, production operations, and application architecture.

### Systems Integration & Automation

Evidence may include current project-backed Linux, Docker, networking, MQTT, RTSP, automation, infrastructure, service and container troubleshooting, plus carefully qualified historical evidence involving telemetry, controls, automated test systems, equipment software, data acquisition, hardware/software interfaces, and field integration.

### Technical Systems Experience

Career-backed evidence includes avionics, communications, radar/GPS/weather systems, RF/electronic measurement, corporate-aircraft avionics work, automated electronic board-test systems, semiconductor fabrication equipment, instrumentation, installations, upgrades, troubleshooting, equipment support, and customer/technical-team coordination where already evidenced.

These pillars are complementary, but they do not erase evidence-source distinctions.

## Current Skills Versus Historical Strengths

Project-backed skills remain separate from transferable career strengths. Historical training or historical exposure does not automatically become a current skill. Later slices may change prominence, grouping, labels, and information hierarchy, but they must not silently promote historical technologies into current proficiency claims.

## Role-Family Breadth

The portfolio should support broad recruiter interpretation across areas such as:

- test automation;
- infrastructure/platform engineering;
- automation;

- applications/solutions engineering;

This is strategic targeting, not authorization to claim unverified formal titles or qualifications. Compensation targets must not become public portfolio content.

## Remote/Travel Positioning

Remote work is a private selection preference, not the primary public professional identity. The public portfolio should not be optimized around "remote-only" messaging. Later public copy may mention opportunity interest only where useful and separately approved. No travel percentage, availability promise, relocation promise, or geographic constraint should be invented or published by this contract.
